    Etymon

    Words wander. Nice once meant foolish; silly meant blessed; a girl could be a boy. You are one of these words, at the start of its journey, and what you mean today depends on where you go.

    The map is meaning. Each dot is a meaning, and your circle holds the ones you carry. The little dots inside it are your speakers. Each century:

    1. 1Read the clue. Each century has meanings it likes and meanings it doesn't, whatever word carries them. It wants words for two meanings, and the clue hints which. What it wants depends on where your word has got to, so the clues follow your path. A meaning or two are out of fashion: nobody wants to say them, and any word that means them loses speakers. A second clue (red ✕) hints at those. You find out which were which afterwards.
    2. 2Tap a meaning to drift toward it, as far as the dashed ring.
    3. 3Optional: tap + on your circle to broaden a lot, or − to narrow a little. Broad is a safer guess, but it takes centuries to shrink again.
    4. 4Continue to the next century. Speakers walk in from each meaning you carry, far more from wanted ones; some forget you.
    5. 5See how you did. A summary shows what each meaning won or lost you, and why; tap a century on the timeline to see it again. Until the next century passes, the map rings what it wanted in gold (filled if you carried it, or with the speakers you missed round it), and crosses out what went out of fashion. Your move is graded best, good, fair or poor against the best one you had. Finding the best route is the puzzle: play again to beat your score.

    • Every meaning you carry wins +3 speakers, or +30 if the century wanted it.
    • Out of fashion meanings cost 25 each if you carry them that century. If you don't, they cost you nothing.
    • Fads (✦) sprout beside meanings you've carried. They pay +12 the century they appear, and go out of fashion two centuries later.
    • The panel shows what you mean now, the clue for next, and what would happen if you drift where your circle is. ✓ under a century means you meant what the word really meant then. Each past century on the timeline shows what it did to your speakers, green for a best or good move, gold for fair, red for poor.
    • Carry more than three and you're too vague (everything you win is diluted); none and nobody knows what you mean (−20). Every century, 10 people forget you anyway.
    • What you become is up to you. After each century you learn what the word really meant then. Mean that in three of the first five centuries and you'll become what it means today. Stray, and your word finds another ending. You'll learn which with three centuries to go: carry it at the end for +100.

    At the end, see how your journey differed from the word's real one. The best route to your ending stays hidden unless you ask to see it, or match its score.
